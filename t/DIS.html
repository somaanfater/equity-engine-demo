Glossary

Every term this report uses, in plain English, with what it does not tell you (§39).

Accrual ratio
Earnings not backed by cash, as a share of assets. Persistently high accruals are the most-studied warning sign in accounting research (Sloan 1996).
ADV
Average daily volume. How much of the stock trades in a normal day, which bounds how large a position can be entered or exited.
Altman Z
A bankruptcy-risk score from five balance-sheet ratios (Altman 1968). Above about 3 is the safe zone for a manufacturer.
Alpha
Return not explained by the market or by the factor model being used. It is defined relative to a model, so it changes when the model does.
Beneish M
An earnings-manipulation score from eight accounting ratios (Beneish 1999). Above -1.78 is the flag, which is a screen and not a verdict.
Beta
How much the stock has moved for a one-percent move in the benchmark. Measured over a window, so it is a description of the past.
Blume adjustment
Shrinking a measured beta toward one, because high and low betas both drift back over time (Blume 1971).
Comps
Comparable-company analysis: valuing a company at the multiples its peers trade on, which answers “what is this worth to the market”, not “what is this worth”.
Days to cover
Short interest divided by average daily volume: how many days of normal trading it would take the shorts to buy back their position.
DCF
Discounted cash flow. The value of a business as the cash it will produce, discounted for time and risk. Its answer is only as good as its drivers.
Distance to default
How many standard deviations of asset value separate a company from the point where its assets equal its debts (Merton 1974).
DuPont decomposition
Splitting return on equity into margin, asset turnover and leverage, so a rising return can be read as efficiency or as borrowing.
EPV
Earnings power value: the business valued on current earnings with no growth. A floor that does not depend on a forecast (Greenwald).
EV
Enterprise value: the value of the whole business, debt included, before subtracting what the lenders own.
Factor exposure
How much of a return is explained by known drivers - size, value, profitability, momentum - rather than by the company itself (Fama and French).
FCF
Free cash flow: cash from operations less the capital spending needed to keep the business running.
Kelly fraction
The bet size that maximises long-run growth given an edge. A raw Kelly above one says the inputs are too confident, not that leverage is advisable.
LBO ceiling
The most a leveraged buyer could pay and still earn its required return. Another floor under the price, not a forecast.
Market impact
The price move caused by trading itself. Estimated here with a square-root law, which is an approximation and never a quotable cost.
Monte Carlo
Running a valuation thousands of times with drawn inputs, to get a distribution instead of a single number. The distribution inherits the assumptions.
NOPAT
Net operating profit after tax: operating profit as if the company had no debt, which is what a DCF discounts.
Point-in-time
Using only information published on or before the analysis date. Without it a backtest learns from the future and looks brilliant.
Reverse DCF
Solving for the growth or margin the current price implies, which turns “is it cheap” into “what would have to be true”.
ROIC
Return on invested capital. Above the cost of capital, growth creates value; below it, growth destroys value.
Sales-to-capital
How much revenue each unit of invested capital supports, used to turn a growth forecast into a capital-spending forecast (Damodaran).
Sharpe ratio
Return per unit of volatility. It treats upside and downside volatility alike, which flatters strategies that lose rarely and badly.
Short interest
Shares sold short and not yet bought back, reported twice a month by the exchanges with a lag.
Terminal value
The value of everything beyond the forecast window. Where most of a DCF usually sits, which is why its share of the total is checked.
WACC
Weighted average cost of capital: the blended return debt and equity holders require, and the rate a DCF discounts at.
13F
The quarterly holdings report large institutions file 45 days after quarter end. Late, and only institutions.
